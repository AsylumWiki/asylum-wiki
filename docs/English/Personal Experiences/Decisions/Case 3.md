## DECISION

**MEMBER**: These are the reasons for the decision in the refugee claim of [REDACTED], the file number is [REDACTED]. I note for the record that [REDACTED], your name at birth and on your passport and still in our official system is [REDACTED]. However, you now go by the name [REDACTED]. You are a citizen of China, and are claiming refugee protection.

In assessing your case, I have considered and applied Immigration and Refugee Board [Chairperson's Guideline Number 9](https://www.irb-cisr.gc.ca/en/legal-policy/policies/Pages/GuideDir09.aspx), related to Sexual Orientation and Gender Identity and Expression. I have considered your testimony and the other evidence in your case, and I am ready to now render my decision orally.

I find that you are a Convention refugee and I therefore accept your claim.

Your allegations in brief are that you face a serious possibility of persecution in China as a feminine presenting, non-binary individual, or as a transgender woman. You allege that you were born a boy, but came to identify more as female in your childhood. You testified that today you identify most closely as a non-binary person, but also as a transgender woman, as you identify and present yourself as much more female than male.

You allege you suffered discrimination and violence in China, in your school community, as well as from your family, due to your feminine presenting presentation. And you allege that you could not live openly now as a transgender woman in China without putting yourself at risk of persecution and harm. And for those reasons, never actually presented as a transgender woman while you were living there.

Regarding your identity, you presented your valid Chinese passport, a copy of which was seized by the Minister and is before me, and I find this is sufficient to establish our identity on a balance of probabilities.

Regarding nexus, I find there is a link between what you fear and one of the five (5) Convention grounds, and that you make up part of a particular social group, that being transgender women or non-binary individuals in China. I have therefore assessed your claim pursuant to section 96.

Regarding credibility, you gave sworn testimony that starts with the presumption of truth. I find the central issue in your claim is your profile as a transgender woman or non-binary person. You were able to offer spontaneous and detailed testimony on this issue. I find you have established on a balance of probabilities that you are a transgender woman or a non-binary person who has experienced discrimination and harm in China as a result.

You provided numerous pieces of documentation in Exhibit number 5, relating--a number of medical documents from your treatment in Canada, indicating that you are on a program of gender-affirming therapy here. These documents also make reference to you having started gender-affirming care back in China in 2022, though you testified credibly that this was done through non-legitimate sources, through medication that you were able to import from Hong Kong.

It is clear to me from just looking at you on the screen today that you do present in a very feminine way. You testified credibly that you have been doing this since you came to Canada.

Exhibit 5 provided some copies of messages, exchanges with your parents that they show significant conflict with them over your gender identity. And so all of these documents, I have no reason to doubt, assign them all significant weight to establishing that you are a transgender woman or non-binary individual. And this has resulted in conflict back in China, particularly with your parents.

Regarding subjective fear, I did have some concerns in that you came to Canada in 2022, did not make a refugee claim at that time. In fact, you made a return to China in [REDACTED] 2023 and did not make any claim for refugee protection until you came back to Canada after this visit to China. You made this claim roughly a year after you first arrived. So delay in claim, return to China, are concerns for subjective fear.

You testified that when you first came here to Canada, your focus was on your studies. You were unaware of the possibility of refugee protection. You were compelled to return to China in 2023 due to a serious mental health incident or episode where your power to make decisions on your own was essentially taken away from you and your parents were contacted who pressured you to come back to China.

Only on your return to Canada, after this return to China, when talking to a social worker in a hospital here, did you come to realize about the possibility of making a refugee claim. I note that you have never lived openly as a transgender woman in China out of fear, including on your return there in 2023.

I note that there is significant evidence of significant mental health struggles from the time you arrived in Canada on file before me at Exhibit 5. I note that you testified credibly that your fear intensified on your return to China when you were threatened by your father with a knife due to his opposition to your gender identity.

So while delay in claim and your return to China are concerning, I accept that you are not aware of the possibility, that your mental health struggles here likely impugned or hurt your ability to take steps on your own to learn about the refugee program.

You have always concealed your true gender identity in China, which I find is indicative of your subjective fear of living openly as a transgender woman there. This also indicates you were taking steps to stay safe on your return there as you were still remaining essentially closeted.

I accept that you had a little choice but to return to China due to your significant mental health crisis in 2023. And I accept it was a knife attack from your father that really crystallized your fear. And yet you made your claim shortly after you returned to Canada after this incident.

So I find your delay in claim and return to China had been adequately explained. I do not draw a negative inference regarding subjective fear. I find you have established your subjective fear on a balance of probabilities.

Regarding objective basis and well-foundedness of your claim, I find the objective basis--objective evidence before me does support your fear of a return to China. Items [1.8](https://www.government.nl/site/binaries/site-content/collections/documents/2020/07/01/country-of-origin-information-report-china-july-2020/COI+Report+China.pdf), [6.1](https://www.hrw.org/report/2017/11/15/have-you-considered-your-parents-happiness/conversion-therapy-against-lgbt-people) indicate there are no regulations or laws protecting the LGBTQ [2SLGBTQI+] community against discrimination based on their gender identity in China.

Item [6.4](https://outrightinternational.org/sites/default/files/2024-10/Chinese_LGBTQ_Activists_at_Risk_Final.pdf) indicates that the lack of formal safeguards leads to LGBTQ persons being very vulnerable to discrimination, including unemployment, education, healthcare, and access to justice.

The same source indicates that LGBTQ persons need family permission in making decisions about their sexual identity, including a change of gender. This is something to which you testified today. Item [6.6](https://outrightinternational.org/sites/default/files/2022-09/PrecariousProgress_2020_1215.pdf) indicates that while there has been some progress in major cities, this progress is very precarious regarding acceptance of gender diverse people, and discrimination and state repression of individuals with diverse gender identity is still pervasive throughout the country, including discrimination and harassment in schools and the workplace.

Fewer than five (5) percent of LGBTQ persons are fully out publicly in China. Items [2.7](https://freedomhouse.org/country/china/freedom-world/2026), [6.3](https://wepub.org/index.php/IJSSPA/article/view/3206/3494), again, reinforces that individuals with diverse gender identity do face discrimination in employment and healthcare and education. Item [6.2](https://ofpra.gouv.fr/libraries/pdf.js/web/viewer.html?file=/sites/default/files/ofpra_flora/2404_chn_sexual_minorities.pdf) indicates that transgender identity is still considered very much to be a mental illness in China.

Again, sex reassignment surgeries require the consent of families, which is a major obstacle to assessing safe, gender-affirming care in the country. Your own evidence provided at Exhibit 5 includes a Washington Center for Human Rights report, again enforcing that family consent is needed for gender transition. And more than half of transgender individuals face abuse and threats from their parents.

A Williams Institute School of Law report discusses violence against transgender individuals in the country. And also an article from Time Magazine discusses the domestic violence often present for transgender individuals, much like you experienced at the hands of your own family. So I find there is objective evidence to support your fear of return to China, that your fear of harm does have an objective basis. along with your established subjective fear, I find you have established a well-founded fear of persecution in China.

Regarding state protection, Item [6.2](https://ofpra.gouv.fr/libraries/pdf.js/web/viewer.html?file=/sites/default/files/ofpra_flora/2404_chn_sexual_minorities.pdf) indicates that LGBTQ persons do face police mistreatment and are often subjected to systematic persecution by authorities. There are accounts of arrests and forced confessions for LGBTQ persons. And the government subscribes to a general policy of conformity and the application of traditional values, which can lead to state sponsored harm of the LGBTQ persons.

There is no legislation protecting members of the LGBTQ community from homophobic acts or discrimination. And Item [6.2](https://ofpra.gouv.fr/libraries/pdf.js/web/viewer.html?file=/sites/default/files/ofpra_flora/2404_chn_sexual_minorities.pdf) goes on to say that the fear of being outed publicly often prevents people from going to authorities when they face violence in the home or otherwise. Therefore, I find there is clear and convincing evidence that adequate state protection would not be forthcoming to you, and the presumption of state protection has been rebutted.

Regarding internal flight alternative, I note that the evidence indicates that these attitudes, negative attitudes towards transgender individuals exists across of China. Also, you declare a personal particular risk from your parents. As I have discussed earlier, consent from families is often required in order to access gender affirming care. Therefore, were you to attempt to access gender affirming care your location would likely become known to your parents which would put you at further risk from them.

I note that you have lived in China without significant harm outside of your home, however you testified credibly that you lived essentially closeted for all the time you were in China, even on your return there.

I turned to [Guideline 4,](https://www.irb-cisr.gc.ca/en/legal-policy/policies/Pages/GuideDir04.aspx) which indicates it is unreasonable to expect individuals to keep their gender identity or sexuality hidden or secret. It is tantamount to being asking someone to live in hiding.

I find were you to live openly as a non-binary or as a transgender woman in China, as you have been doing in Canada, that you would face a serious possibility of persecution anywhere in the country. And it is not reasonable to expect you to revert to hiding your sexual orientation, or pardon me, to hiding your gender identity in order to be safe. Therefore, I find you do not have a viable internal flight alternative anywhere in China.

So for all these reasons, I find you are a Convention refugee, and I therefore accept your claim.
